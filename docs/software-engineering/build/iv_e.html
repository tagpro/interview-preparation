Part five

System design

The round with no right answer and a great many wrong ones. You are given three sentences and forty-five minutes, and the grade comes from whether you turned the ambiguity into a set of decisions, each with a reason and a cost. The subject matter is finite — a dozen building blocks, a page of numbers, four consistency ideas and a catalogue of problems that reuse them.

Topic · xx

The method, and the clock it runs on

Every good design round has the same six phases in the same order. Skipping the first two is the most common way to fail, and it is invisible until minute thirty when nothing can be justified.

  1. Functional requirements, written as a list. Three to five bullet points, agreed out loud. “Shorten a URL, resolve one, expire them, count clicks — is analytics in scope?”
  2. Non-functional requirements, with numbers. Scale, latency target, availability target, consistency needs, retention, cost sensitivity. Ask for them; invent them if the interviewer will not give them; either way, write them down.
  3. Envelope estimates. Requests per second at peak, storage per year, bandwidth. They decide whether one machine or a hundred, and everything after depends on that answer.
  4. The interface. The endpoints or the events, with request and response shapes. This is the contract the rest of the design serves.
  5. The data model. Tables or documents, the primary key, the access patterns, and the index that supports each one.
  6. The diagram, then one request walked through it. Draw it, then narrate a single write and a single read from the client to storage and back.
  7. Bottleneck, then scale it. Find the first thing that breaks, fix that one thing, then look again. Scaling everything at once is not a plan.
  8. Failure and operations. Each component dies in turn: what does the user see, what recovers it, what do you alert on. Finish here even if you have to cut something else short.

Consistency, ordering and delivery

Four ideas that decide most of the hard follow-up questions: what a reader is promised, what a partition forces, what a message queue really guarantees, and how you make a repeated write harmless.

Strong consistencyEvery read sees the latest committed write. Costs coordination, and therefore latency and availability during a partition.
Eventual consistencyReads converge if writes stop. Fine for a like count, wrong for a bank balance — say which yours is.
Read-your-writesThe weakest guarantee that stops users thinking the product is broken. Usually the one you actually need.
Monotonic readsTime never goes backwards for one reader. Broken by round-robin routing across lagging replicas.
Causal consistencyEffects never appear before causes. The right model for comments, replies and threads.
CAP and PACELCDuring a partition, choose consistency or availability; when there is no partition, choose latency or consistency. The second half is the everyday one.
QuorumsR + W > N gives overlap. Tunable per request, which is why some stores let you choose per call.
Linearisability vs serialisabilityOne is about single objects in real time, the other about transactions in some order. Not the same word twice.
At-most-once, at-least-once, exactly-onceThe third does not exist on the wire. It is at-least-once delivery plus idempotent processing, and saying so is the answer.
OrderingGlobal order is expensive; per-key order is cheap and usually sufficient. Partition by the key you need ordered.
IdempotencyA client-supplied key, a uniqueness constraint, and a stored response. Three lines that make retries safe.
ClocksWall clocks drift and jump; use monotonic clocks for durations, logical or hybrid clocks for ordering.
Distributed locksLeases with fencing tokens. A lock without a fencing token does not survive a paused process.
ConsensusRaft or Paxos, and where it hides: leader election, config stores, and the coordination you should try not to need.
The dual writeWriting to a database and a queue without a transaction. Always broken; the outbox is the standard fix.
Conflict resolutionLast write wins, version vectors, CRDTs — and the fact that last write wins silently loses data.

The problem catalogue, and what each one is really testing

The list of questions asked is short and stable. Each one exists to force a specific decision — recognise the decision and the rest of the answer assembles itself.

ProblemThe decision it exists to force
URL shortenerKey generation without coordination, and a read path that is almost pure cache.
Pastebin / file shareWhere the bytes live versus where the metadata lives, and signed URLs.
Rate limiterWhich algorithm, and where the counter lives when there are fifty API servers.
News feed / timelineFan-out on write against fan-out on read, and the celebrity who breaks whichever you chose.
Chat / messagingConnection state, delivery receipts, ordering per conversation, and offline devices.
Notification serviceFan-out, deduplication, per-channel retries, and user preferences as a filter.
Typeahead / autocompletePrecomputation, a trie or an index, and a latency budget of tens of milliseconds.
Web crawlerPoliteness, frontier scheduling, deduplication at enormous scale, and traps.
Search engineInverted index, ranking, sharding by document, and index freshness.
Video streamingTranscoding pipeline, adaptive bitrate, CDN economics, and cold storage of originals.
Cloud drive / syncChunking, deduplication, conflict resolution, and delta sync over a bad network.
Ride hailingGeospatial indexing, matching under contention, and location updates as a write flood.
Food deliveryThree-sided state machine, and the fact that the real world can reject your transaction.
Ticket bookingOverselling. Holds, expiry, and a transaction boundary you can defend.
Payments / ledgerDouble-entry, idempotency, exactly-once as an application property, and reconciliation.
Digital walletBalance as a derived value, not a mutable field. Auditability over convenience.
Ad click aggregationStreaming aggregation, late events, watermarks, and approximate versus exact counts.
Metrics / monitoringTime-series storage, cardinality, downsampling, and query cost at read time.
Log aggregationIngest volume, retention tiers, and search over data you cannot afford to index fully.
Distributed cachePlacement, consistent hashing, eviction, and what happens when a node leaves.
Key–value storeReplication, quorums, conflict resolution, and the storage engine underneath.
Object storeDurability arithmetic, erasure coding, and metadata as the real scaling problem.
Job schedulerExactly-once triggering, clock skew, long-running work, and failure mid-job.
LeaderboardSorted sets, approximate ranks at scale, and time-windowed resets.
Collaborative editorOperational transformation or CRDTs, and presence as a separate problem.
Stock exchangeA single-threaded matching engine, determinism, and why sharding is the wrong instinct here.
Proximity searchGeohashing or quadtrees, and the query that crosses a cell boundary.
Recommendation feedOffline candidate generation, online ranking, and a latency budget split between them.
A/B testing platformDeterministic bucketing, exposure logging, and not letting analysis change the assignment.
Feature flagsPropagation delay, fail-open defaults, and a service that must never be down.
