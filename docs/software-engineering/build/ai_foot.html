How to use this page. It is written for an engineer with no AI experience and real engineering judgement, which is the position most people are actually interviewing from. Read a topic’s three-cell strip first — the problem, the mechanism, what to say — and you have the shape of an answer. Read the levels and the code when you want the substance behind it. The fifteen figures are the part worth spending time on: drag the sliders until something surprises you, because a number you have watched move is a number you can reason about under pressure, and a number you have only read is not.

The code. Every snippet appears in Python, TypeScript and Go, and the switch in the bar (or p / t / g) changes all of them at once and remembers your choice. The three are not translations of each other: Python takes the model and data side, TypeScript the product edge where streaming and degradation live, and Go the service layer with its budgets, deadlines and retries — because that is how these systems are actually built. Snippets that call a model use the Anthropic SDKs and current model identifiers; everything else is deliberately dependency-free so the mechanism is visible rather than hidden behind a framework.

On the numbers. Prices are the published August 2026 list rates for Claude Opus 5 and Claude Haiku 4.5, and they will move — check them before quoting any of this, and notice that none of the arithmetic changes when they do. Model shapes in the memory figures come from published configurations. Latency and throughput figures are orders of magnitude, quoted to show a shape rather than to be a measurement of your system. Where this page says a technique “usually” helps, that is an invitation to measure it on your own data, which is the single habit that separates this field’s practitioners from its commentators.
